## THE THREAD IN ONE SENTENCE

Garran Hill belongs to the same building moment as Pinehurst itself — Neo-Georgian brick, shaped by a craftsman world that Leonard Tufts built two miles away, completed in 1916 as the war consumed its patron, and inscribed at the threshold with the name it still carries.

## WHAT IS VERIFIED — SOURCE BY SOURCE

### A. "Page hired an architect, who set to work designing a two-story Georgian brick house."
**Source:** PineStraw, "A Page Out of History"
**Status:** ✓ VERIFIED — direct quote from article citing The Pinehurst Outlook
**Public-safe:** YES
**Confirmed detail:** The design was a two-story Georgian brick house. Architect identity not named.

### B. "Construction was soon underway."
**Source:** PineStraw, "A Page Out of History"
**Status:** ✓ VERIFIED — direct quote
**Note:** This confirms construction began in 1913. No specific contractor named in source.

### C. Ralph Page supervised construction.
**Source:** PineStraw "A Page Out of History" (Ralph and Frank in Moore County; Ralph made the farm his residence); Village Heritage Foundation corroborates Ralph ownership through 1940
**Status:** ✓ VERIFIED — Ralph's role in the project is source-confirmed
**Public-safe:** YES — "Construction supervised by Ralph Page" is an approved line per Source Fact Audit

### D. Leonard Tufts / craftsmen / Pinehurst builders.
**Source:** DEC-008 brief ingest — Rachel's compilation; COPY_DOCUMENT_V3/V4/V5 — all versions include this line
**External corroboration:** PineStraw "A Page Out of History" confirms the setting — Pinehurst is "the town formed out of Moore County pine barrens by James Walker Tufts." Village Heritage Foundation confirms Leonard Tufts removed top floors of the Magnolia Inn in 1914 — active in Pinehurst construction during the exact period of Garran Hill's build. Leonard authored the 1906 Pinehurst promotional book.
**Status:** ✓ PLAUSIBLE WITH STRONG CONTEXT — not a smoking-gun citation but deeply consistent with documented facts. Leonard Tufts was operationally active in Pinehurst construction 1895–1916. Garran Hill built two miles from village center in 1913–1916.
**Public-safe:** YES with careful wording. "Craftsmen from Leonard Tufts' Pinehurst building world" — in-sentence, not a headline claim.
**Note:** See guardrails section. Do NOT say "the same men who built the Carolina Hotel" — that specific claim is not source-confirmed.

### E. "The hunt for matching ones took three months." (Brick restoration)
**Source:** PineStraw "Story of a House" — exact quote
**Context in full:** *"Workmen ran into trouble when replacing the front portico, deemed wrong for the era. During its removal, bricks fell off the house front. The hunt for matching ones took three months."*
**Status:** ✓ VERIFIED — direct quote from PineStraw
**Public-safe:** YES
**Confirmed detail:** The original brick has a specific composition that required three months of sourcing to match during the 1999–2001 restoration. This is a significant architectural fact — it establishes the brick as authorial, not generic.

### F. Carolina Hotel visible from front porch because land was cleared.
**Source:** Village Heritage Foundation, "Four Village Landmarks Honored"
**Full quote:** *"It boasted a view of the Carolina Hotel, the dairy and the golf course. If you've ever ridden out there, you don't see very far this way anymore so you can imagine how barren the land was."* — attributed to Laura Byers, Moore County resident and Historic Plaque Committee member
**Status:** ✓ VERIFIED — third-party attributed quote in a published source
**Public-safe:** YES — this is a historical observation about the land's clearance, not a construction claim
**Note:** This also confirms the Carolina Hotel existed and was visible, but does NOT confirm the same craftsmen built both.

### G. GARRAN HILL · 1916 threshold inscription.

### H. Built in 1915–16.
**Source:** Village Heritage Foundation — "the Georgian country manor house was finished in 1916"

### I. Neo-Georgian style.
**Source:** PineStraw "Story of a House" — "simple and dignified" formal manor character; PineStraw "A Page Out of History" — "two-story Georgian brick house"

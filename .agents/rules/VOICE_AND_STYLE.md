## THE ONE-LINE

We write seductively, but with truth.

---

## WHAT THIS PAGE IS FOR

This is a for-sale property listing. $4.25M. Sotheby's International Realty.

The page exists to do three things:

1. Make a qualified buyer feel something no MLS listing can deliver.
2. Earn their trust on facts (price, beds, baths, square feet, history, restoration).
3. Move them to request a private showing.

If a sentence, headline, image, or section doesn't serve one of those three jobs, it doesn't belong on the page.

---

## THE BUYER

The reader is an adult. They have money. They have taste. They have other options.

They are most likely reading this page for the first time on their phone: forwarded by an agent, sent by a spouse, found on Instagram. Scrolling in bed, between meetings, in an airport.

They'll decide in 15 seconds whether this page is worth their attention. If it falls apart on mobile, they close the tab. They don't go get their laptop.

Desktop is the *second* read. Mobile is the first. Mobile is where the seduction has to work.

---

## SEDUCTIVE, BUT WITH TRUTH

**Seductive** means we do not reach. We do not declare. We place a single detail on the page and stop talking. The buyer does the work. That's what makes them fall in love.

**With truth** means every flourish is anchored to a fact. If you can't point to which part of the house, which year, which name — cut it.

At $4.25M, a buyer will not tolerate either without the other. Pure poetry feels manipulative. Pure facts feel like every other listing. The two together are what get the private showing request.

Examples (all currently live):

- "Fire going. No one home yet." — not "A romantic drawing room."
- "They made it bigger so they could dance." — not "Spacious living room."
- "Someone still puts flowers there." — not "Immaculately maintained."
- "The door has been open since 1916." — not "Historic 100+-year-old residence."

The seduction is in the unsaid part. The truth is what makes it believable.

---

## THE SEVEN RULES

**1. One detail is enough.** One specific thing, placed well, does more work than three adjectives.

**2. Short sentences carry weight.** Especially true on mobile.

**3. Put the emotion in the physical detail.** Don't tell the buyer how to feel. Show them something real.
- Weak: "This is a home where memories are made."
- Strong: "Every morning at 6:30, the deer come through."

**4. Italics have to earn it.** Italics signal a moment. Headline turn, locked quote, pulled line. Not decoration.

**5. The house is the subject.** Not the realtor. Not the buyer. Not the sellers. The house.

**6. Trust the buyer.** Don't explain what they already know. Get out of their way.

**7. No reach.** If a line is reaching for a feeling it hasn't earned, the buyer feels it and pulls back. Every sentence should stand still.

---

## RESPONSIVE (MOBILE-FIRST) RULES

Most buyers see this page first on a 375px iPhone. Every headline, pulled line, and section must work at that width.

**1. Headlines are tested at 375px.** Our pattern (clause + italic turn) works well because the two halves stack cleanly as two lines. If an italic turn gets orphaned on a phone, rewrite.

**2. Pulled lines must survive stacking.** On desktop, a pulled line may sit in white space next to a photo. On mobile it sits *under* the photo. It must still land as a beat.

**3. Every section needs an eyebrow + headline.** Without these anchors, a section floats into whatever's around it on mobile and disappears. *(This is why the current intro (Page/Dumaine/Restoration) looks unanchored.)*

**4. Two-paragraph max per room.** Most rooms need one paragraph. The three hero rooms (Living, Drawing, Library) get two.

**5. Image galleries collapse.** A 4-up desktop grid becomes a 1-column mobile stack. Copy introducing a gallery shouldn't depend on images being adjacent.

**6. CTAs must be reachable.** "Request Private Showing" appears near the top and again at the close. Ideally a sticky mobile footer button too.

**7. Stat bar needs space.** "4Bedrooms" is a CSS bug on mobile breakpoints. Correct format:

```
4 / Bedrooms
4 Full · 2 Powder / Baths
6,072 sq ft / Living Area
4.15 Acres / Land
$4,250,000 / Offered At
```

## CONVERSION RULES

- **The CTA: Request Private Showing.** Never "Contact Us." Never "Learn More." Never "Schedule a Tour."
- **The price: $4,250,000 visible 3×.** Hero stat bar, close, footer. Never hidden. Never a range. Price qualifies the buyer before they request a showing.
- **The address: 200 Hollycrest Drive.** Hero, close, footer.
- **Sotheby's IR: footer.** Part of the trust package. The buyer is assessing you as much as the house.
- **Matterport:** Link it once (Threshold/Entry section is ideal, after the buyer is "inside"). 3D tour is a qualifier — serious buyers click.
- **Blueprints/documentation:** Named in Restoration: "Fifteen architectural drawings document every decision O'Shea made. They survive. They transfer with the property." A trust signal no other listing can match.
- **Privacy posture:** "All inquiries held in strict confidence" near the inquiry form.

## HARD RULES (from MASTER BRIEF)

These are sales rules as much as editorial rules. Never break:

- NEVER name current stewards/owners.
- NEVER use "he never walked through the door" — not verified.
- NEVER market tennis courts — deteriorated.
- Price / Beds / Baths / Acres LOCKED — MLS sync.
- Hero video is a WIP — do not touch.
- Deploy via GitHub only — NEVER Base44 publish.
- David Prest named ONCE (Library only).
- Betty Dumaine name encouraged.
- Spelling: Garran Hill (never Garren). Dumaine (never Demaine).

---

## BANNED WORDS

```
stunning · nestled · charming · boasts · elegant · cozy · spacious · beautiful
luxurious · features (as verb) · offers · provides · showcases · remarkable
impressive · breathtaking · magnificent · pristine · oasis · sanctuary · escape · dream (adj)
```

Also avoid: story, storied, historic, heritage, timeless, iconic, unparalleled.
The facts carry these meanings on their own.

## HEADLINE PATTERN

Every section follows the same anatomy:

**Eyebrow.** Small, gold, uppercase, letter-spaced. A quiet label that says where you are.
*Ex: THE LIVING ROOM*

**Headline.** A clause and a turn. The turn goes in italics.
*Ex: They made it bigger so they could dance.*

**Body.** One paragraph for facts. For hero rooms, a second for what those facts feel like.

This anatomy works on desktop *the same way* it works on mobile because each part is structurally distinct.

---

## THE "SEDUCTIVE BUT TRUE" CHECK

Before any line ships:

1. Can I point to what in the house this is about? *(truth)*
2. Is there a detail the buyer can see, touch, or hear? *(seductive)*
3. Am I trusting them to feel it, or telling them what to feel? *(seductive)*
4. Does it read cleanly on a 375px phone? *(responsive)*
5. Does this help the buyer request a showing? *(sales)*
6. Am I reaching? *(if yes, cut)*
7. Have I used a banned word? *(if yes, cut)*
8. Could a shorter sentence do the same work? *(if yes, rewrite)*

If you're ever not sure, re-read these. Write to them.

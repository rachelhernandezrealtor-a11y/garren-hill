The History  ·  Walter Hines Page

He named it Garran Hill.
He sailed for London.

He bought the land in February 1913. Named it Garran Hill. Six weeks later, Woodrow Wilson appointed him Ambassador to the Court of St. James.

He sailed for London. Into the war.

The house was completed without him. His son Ralph supervised. Leonard Tufts' craftsmen built it. From London, through four years, he wrote home about one thing.

"The farm — the farm — the farm." The Life and Letters of Walter H. Page  ·  Vol. I, P. 354  ·  1923

He came home in December 1918. Carried from the train at Aberdeen. He died ten days later.

He co-founded Doubleday, Page & Co. He edited The Atlantic Monthly. He nominated Woodrow Wilson for the presidency.

He never spent a night here.

Source: PineStraw Magazine "A Page Out of History" · October 2019

Blue Fox  ·  Betty Dumaine's Irish Hunter  ·  Buried on the grounds

1959 – 1984

Some places remember
who loved them.

Elizabeth Dumaine purchased the property in 1959. She was a foxhunter. She added stables, kennels, and an enclosure for peacocks — known to be guard birds. She changed the name to Hollycrest.

Her favorite horse is buried on the grounds.

The Restoration  ·  1999–2001

Beauty was not enough.
The house needed discipline.

In 1999, Thomas O'Shea drew the house from scratch. Every room measured. Every cornice profiled. Every proportion committed to paper before a single board was replaced.

Contractor Dennis Dunagan spent three years executing those drawings.

All seven carved mantels saved. Original oak flooring lifted, repaired, relaid. Period-accurate portico brick — three months to source. Custom Marvin windows made to the 1916 profile.

It does not feel added. It feels inevitable.

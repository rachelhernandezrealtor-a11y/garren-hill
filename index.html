The Estate  ·  Neo-Georgian

Then the house appears.Some houses hold history.
This one shaped it.

The door has been open since 1916.

Flemish bond brick. White portico. A circular lawn held before the entrance like a pause.

Built 1915–16. Four columns. Original brick. The same hands that built Pinehurst.

Garran Hill  ·  1916

The door has been opensince 1916.

The inscription is cut into the stone above the entry: Garran Hill, 1916. It was placed there by the craftsmen Leonard Tufts sent -- the same men building Pinehurst. It has not been altered. It has not been restored. It simply remains.

The History  ·  Walter Hines Page

He named it Garran Hill.He never spent a night here.

He bought the land in February 1913. Named it Garran Hill. Six weeks later, Woodrow Wilson appointed him Ambassador to the Court of St. James.

He sailed for London. Into the war.

The house was completed without him. His son Ralph supervised. From London, through four years, he wrote home about one thing.

"The farm -- the farm -- the farm."

Walter Hines Page  ·  The Life and Letters of Walter H. Page, Vol. I, P. 354  ·  1923

Blue Fox  ·  Betty Dumaine's Irish Hunter  ·  Buried on the Grounds

1959 – 1984

Some places rememberwho loved them.

Elizabeth Dumaine purchased the property in 1959. She was a foxhunter. She added stables, kennels, and an enclosure for peacocks -- known to be guard birds.

Her favorite horse is buried on the grounds.

The Restoration
The Restoration  ·  1999–2001

Beauty was not enough.The house needed discipline.

In 1999, Thomas O'Shea drew the house from scratch. Every room measured. Every cornice profiled. Every proportion committed to paper before a single board was replaced.

All seven carved mantels saved. Original oak flooring lifted, repaired, relaid. Period-accurate portico brick -- three months to source. Custom Marvin windows made to the 1916 profile.

It does not feel added. It feels inevitable.

National Register  ·  Criterion B

Every claim is sourced.Every fact is verified.

The Pines Preservation Guild visited April 2024. The property qualifies under Criterion B -- significant association with a figure of national importance.
